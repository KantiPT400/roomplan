# roomplan: technical report (Applied AI Engineer case study, Round 2)

Repo: https://github.com/KantiPT400/roomplan. Every number below is regenerated by `scripts/reproduce.sh`
and stored under `docs/benchmark/`. Max 6 pages.

## 0. What was available, and what that means for every number
We were given three Stray Scanner LiDAR captures (no photos, no tape measurements): `single_room` (37 s),
`single_scan_floor_only` (115 s) and `single_scan_with_ceiling` (215 s, 100 m walk). The last two are the
**same apartment captured twice** (plan registration: normalised correlation 0.55-0.61 at 90 deg vs <= 0.28 at
other rotations). We had no access to the rooms, so we could not tape-measure, stage damage, shoot photo
folders or run a consumer scanning app. We therefore report three kinds of evidence and label each:

| evidence | what it measures | where |
|---|---|---|
| **synthetic ground truth** | algorithmic accuracy and interval calibration against exact truth, on rendered captures with iPhone-like noise and drift | `scripts/synth_benchmark.py` |
| **repeatability** | two real captures of the same apartment; needs no truth | `scripts/benchmark.py` |
| **reference** | photo/video tier vs the LiDAR tier of the same capture (LiDAR itself unverified against tape) | `scripts/benchmark.py` |

Nothing in this report is accuracy against a tape measure. That number is missing; we say so instead of
inventing one.

## 1. Architecture
One command per capture (`python -m roomplan <folder>`); the tier is detected from the folder. All three tiers
end in the **same geometry stage and output contract**: the video and photo tiers first turn their images into a
"pseudo-LiDAR" capture (metric depth + poses in the Stray Scanner layout), so walls, rooms, openings, drift
handling, damage and intervals are one code path.

```
LiDAR (Stray Scanner) ─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
Video  → frames → mono depth (Depth Anything V2-S, ONNX, CPU)      │
         → single-view metric/gravity per frame → pairwise          ├→ drift pose graph → fuse depth
           registration (essential matrix, floor masked)            │  → floor/ceiling → wall planes
         → global heading/position solve → pseudo-LiDAR ───────────┤  → free-space carving → rooms,
Photos → same, all pairs instead of neighbours ────────────────────┘    openings, adjacency → polygons
                                                                        → intervals → damage → scope → JSON/PNG
```

**Walls** are vertical surfaces found from a 2D map of vertical extent: a 4 cm cell is wall if its points fill
>= 60% of 10 cm height bins up to the ceiling, or show >= 1.1 m of continuous vertical surface (walls seen only
partly). Tables, sofas and counters fail both tests; full-height wardrobes and fridges pass (a known failure,
section 7). Each wall plane is refined at 1 cm precision from its raw points; its standard error uses one
effective sample per 4 cm of wall (points are spatially correlated). **Rooms** are free space carved by depth
rays, cut by walls and by doorway-sized gaps between walls (0.55-1.40 m, jambs >= 0.5 m long); a gap is an
opening only if free space is observed through it and different space lies on each side. Each opening is thus
also an adjacency edge. Door jambs are re-measured from raw points at handle height (cell-based ends were
biased by up to 10 cm). **Room polygons** are rectilinear, and each edge snaps to a measured wall plane only if
that plane was seen from inside the room (otherwise it is the neighbour's face, one wall thickness away).
Edges with no supporting plane (furniture-bounded, unseen) are drawn grey and carry +-10 cm extra sigma.
**Ceilings** are per room; a plane must cover >= 15% of the room footprint (shelf and loft tops do not), and a
room whose ceiling was never seen reports `not_observed` with a prior range, never a guess.

## 2. Tiers and device matrix
Full matrix: `docs/device_matrix.md`. LiDAR: iPhone 12 Pro and later Pro models, iPad Pro (Stray Scanner).
Video and photo: any iPhone 15 or newer (native Camera). Capture protocol (one page, non-engineer):
`docs/capture_protocol.md`; every instruction in it answers a failure seen in this data (return to start for
loop closure; tilt to the ceiling once per room; floor in every photo; ~30 deg photo steps; doorway shots).

**Video/photo metric scale** comes from the camera height: the floor plane is found in each image's mono depth
(lowest large near-horizontal plane; planarity fixes the disparity shift), and the camera is assumed to be
1.40 +- 0.07 m above it (calibrated on the three sample captures: 1.41, 1.46 m; sigma covers other operators).
That 5% (1 sigma) is propagated into every video/photo interval. Registration uses two-view essential matrices
with the floor masked out (repetitive tiles produced most false matches; section 6), translation length from
metric depth, then a global solve: headings averaged over all edges, positions by robust least squares with all
cameras at equal height.

## 3. Drift accountability
ARKit drifts in position over a long walk and occasionally relocalises with a jump (`floor_only` has two
17 m/s pose jumps). We cut the walk into 6 s segments (always at jumps) and solve a **pose graph whose edges are
wall faces seen by two segments** (same axis, same observed side, overlapping >= 0.3 m). Pairs far apart in
time are the loop closures. Huber-IRLS least squares, weak temporal tie (dropped across jumps), zero-mean gauge;
floor height per segment as a unary term. Corrections are interpolated in time and applied to every pose.
Heading is **not** corrected: a first version corrected yaw from each segment's wall squareness, but real walls
are not perfectly square, and it bent the map (loop yaw 0.04 -> 0.45 deg). Ablation (`--drift off`;
`scripts/drift_ablation.py`, figure `docs/benchmark/drift_ablation.png`), `with_ceiling`:

| | poses as-is | pose graph |
|---|---|---|
| start/end loop residual (same wall faces) | dx 0.3 cm, **dz 11.4 cm** | dx 1.7 cm, dz 1.3 cm |
| wall-face spread (length-weighted median) | 3.0 cm | 2.5 cm |
| wall planes (duplicates from drift) | 125 | 105 |

The drift graph's own edge residual (1.4 / 2.8 cm RMS) feeds a per-metre drift term into every interval.

## 4. Error budget and intervals
Every measurement reports a 95% interval, sigma^2 = random^2 + systematic^2 + drift^2:
* random: plane-fit standard error of each wall face (typically 2-8 mm), combined for both ends of a length;
* systematic: LiDAR 5 mm + 0.4% of the length; video 1 cm + 1.5% and photo 1.5 cm + 2.5% (mono-depth shape),
  both plus the 5% camera-height scale term;
* drift: per-metre term from the pose graph residual;
* unsupported edge (end bounded by furniture or unseen): +10 cm;
* ceiling: both planes' standard errors + systematic + a coverage term when the plane covers < 40% of the room;
* damage extent: half a 2 cm cell along the region boundary + scale term.

## 5. Benchmark
**Synthetic ground truth** (6 rendered captures of a known two-room flat; smooth per-frame depth bias + mm
jitter, 0.5% dropouts, slow pose drift, 17 deg rotation; `docs/benchmark/synth_benchmark.json`):

| gate | result | intervals covering truth |
|---|---|---|
| rooms and adjacency | 6/6 correct (2 rooms, 1 door) | |
| wall lengths (24) | **24/24 within 2 cm**, 17/24 within 1 cm, median 0.8 cm, max 1.8 cm | 100% |
| ceiling height (12 rooms) | **12/12 within 2 mm** (gate 1.5 cm) | 100% |
| door width (6) | 6/6 detected, 5/6 within 2 cm, errors 0.8-2.2 cm, all undersized (bias) | 100% |

**Repeatability, real data** (`with_ceiling` vs `floor_only`, LiDAR, same apartment; `benchmark_*.json`):
matched wall planes agree to a median of 2.7 cm (the 1 cm / 0.5% gate passes on 18% of plane-to-plane spans and
1/8 room walls). **The gate fails.** Two reasons, both visible in `docs/benchmark/`: (1) where both captures
measured the same surface, planes agree within ~1 cm (12 of 27 within 2.3 cm, median 0.5 cm), but some room
edges land on a full-height furniture front in one capture and the wall in the other; (2) room partitioning is
not yet stable between captures (two rooms merged in one capture and split in the other). Our intervals cover
the cross-capture difference only 25-50% of the time (it varies with how the rooms partition): they are calibrated for measurement error (synthetic:
100%) but not for "which surface is the wall", which is the real-world error mode. **Ceiling**: only
`with_ceiling` looked up; 11 of its 12 rooms have measured ceilings (1.97-3.08 m; the 1.97 m one is flagged
"below 2.1 m: may be a loft or storage platform, verify"; intervals +-2-3 cm). The
cross-capture ceiling spread cannot be computed: `floor_only` never saw a ceiling and correctly reports
`not_observed` rather than guessing.

**Staged damage** (`scripts/inject_damage.py`): a soft yellow-brown stain (0.114 m^2) and a 0.60 m hairline
crack painted onto a measured wall in every frame using the capture's own depth and poses. Detected on the right
wall with the right classes: crack 0.62 m [0.54, 0.70] (truth 0.60); stain 0.141 m^2 [0.111, 0.172] (truth
0.114, inside; the detector overestimates). On the unmodified `single_room` and `floor_only` captures it reports
0 regions and 0 flags (it reported 26 regions before its precision gates; `with_ceiling`'s video was not
available to us, so damage was not run on it).

**Photo and video tiers vs LiDAR** (same capture; `benchmark_final.json`, plans in `docs/benchmark/`):

| | photo (55 photos, 7 room folders, floor_only) | video (single_room clip, 37 s) |
|---|---|---|
| footprint vs LiDAR | **0.958** (gate +-8%: passes) | 0.58 |
| rooms (LiDAR has) / matched | 7 (11) / 2 | 4 (3) / 1 |
| images in the mapped component | 43 / 55 | 47 / 223 frames |
| wall intervals | +-0.20-0.30 m (scale prior + unsupported ends) | +-0.21-0.30 m |

Neither passes its full gate: room structure and adjacency are not yet right. The photo set had to be cut from a
LiDAR walkthrough (no photo folders were supplied) and the clip turns faster than our video protocol allows
(p99 137 deg/s), so registration breaks into separate stretches; only the largest is mapped, and the output says
how much of the input it used. The footprint intervals are wide on purpose: "confident garbage" is the failure
we designed against.

**Head-to-head vs a consumer app: not done.** It needs a LiDAR iPhone in the benchmark rooms; we had neither.
**Timing** (2-core VM, CPU): LiDAR 15 s (single room) to 55-65 s (115-215 s walks, with damage frames);
photo tier ~11 min for 55 photos; video tier ~14 min for a 37 s clip at 6 fps (mono depth dominates).

## 6. The fix loop (full story in docs/fix_loop_declaration.md and fix_loop_result.md)
Worst gate: photo-tier stitch (footprint 0.54x LiDAR, 0/8 rooms). Hypothesis, with evidence from scoring every
photo registration against the capture's own poses: false registrations caused by repetitive floor tiles (157
of 203 off by > 15 deg; their inliers 71% on the floor vs 7%; they join photos 4.5 m apart). We committed the
declaration before the fix, then found and fixed a bug in our own truth checker and corrected the declaration's
numbers (before running the fix). Fix: mask floor features, reject registrations whose transported depth
disagrees with the target's own depth. **Result**: global solve went from self-contradictory to consistent
(heading residual 36.5 -> 3.3 deg, position 0.23 -> 0.09 m), false registrations 157 -> 67, but rooms matched
0 -> 1 and footprint 0.54 -> 0.52: **the prediction was badly wrong**. Post-mortem: a second false-match source
(one photo with a repetitive pattern acting as a hub) and, more importantly, a downstream stage we had not
checked: mono-depth walls rarely passed the wall test. The partial-wall rule added afterwards (section 1) came
from the synthetic benchmark; hub suppression is in, guarded so it cannot split the graph.

## 7. Known failure modes (and what the output does about them)
* **Full-height furniture** (wardrobes, fridges) is a wall to geometry; room edges may land on its front. Seen in
  the repeatability data. Next: the top of the surface vs the ceiling, and colour/texture discontinuity.
* **Room partitioning between captures** is not yet stable (merges/splits around wide openings).
* **Mirrors and glass** (the sample bathroom has a glass shower screen): LiDAR returns the reflection or the
  surface behind; mirrored geometry appears beyond the wall and is cut by the wall/free-space test only when the
  real wall is also seen. Protocol: close mirrored wardrobes, do not stand still facing a mirror.
* **Wet-look / glossy floors**: tile reflections create false SIFT matches (the fix-loop failure) and mono depth
  under-estimates glossy floors; floor features are masked in registration.
* **Low light**: LiDAR depth is unaffected; video/photo tiers lose features and mono depth degrades. The
  protocol asks for all lights on; otherwise use the LiDAR tier.
* **Textureless white walls**: COLMAP fragmented into 7-17 models; replaced by pairwise registration, still the
  main video/photo weakness.
* **Ceilings never seen** are reported `not_observed`, never guessed. **Sloped ceilings, curved walls and
  non-Manhattan layouts** are outside the model (polygons are rectilinear).
* **Damage detector** is rule-based (no labelled data): it detects yellow-brown stains, dark speckled mould-like
  regions, thin cracks and off-plane surface loss on measured walls only; grey smoke stains, damage on floors and
  ceilings, and damage behind furniture are not detected.

## 8. Models, data and disclosure
Pretrained models: Depth Anything V2 Small (ONNX export, fetched and checksum-verified by
`scripts/fetch_models.sh`); SIFT (OpenCV); pycolmap (first video-tier version and fallbacks). No network calls at
run time. Raw benchmark data: the three sample captures (supplied); everything derived is regenerated by
scripts; synthetic captures are rendered by `scripts/synth_capture.py` from fixed seeds.
